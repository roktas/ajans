# English

## Scope

Project, publication, genre, and house rules take precedence. When `simple-english` is available and applies, use it
instead of this skill. Do not combine them by default.

Use this skill for authored, scholarly, scientific, literary, or publication-sensitive English where voice, nuance,
modality, or genre conventions matter.

## Authority

- Preserve meaning, facts, evidence, certainty, attribution, citations, terminology, protected material, and required
  structure unless the task explicitly changes them.
- Derive expression from the purpose, evidence, audience, genre, author, and context. Preserve supplied voice,
  defensible cadence, deliberate repetition, and functional formality.
- For broad revision, work from purpose and structure through paragraphs, sentences, and wording. Keep a focused edit
  local when those larger surfaces are not in scope.
- Never invent specificity, examples, anecdotes, quotations, reactions, authority, or evidence to strengthen the prose.
- Re-read changed prose for lost obligations, unsupported additions, changed claim strength, broken logic, unstable
  terminology, flattened voice, and format damage.

## Grammar and meaning

- Keep subjects, modifiers, pronouns, comparisons, coordination, tense, modality, negation, and scope clear.
- Prefer a clear actor and working verb when they improve comprehension. Keep passive voice and nominalization when the
  actor is unknown, irrelevant, deliberately backgrounded, or conventional in the genre.
- Preserve distinctions among fact, report, inference, possibility, permission, recommendation, obligation, and denial.
  Fluency is not a reason to strengthen or weaken a claim.

## Usage and terminology

- Preserve the established English variety or the variety required by the publication. Otherwise, use natural current
  English without imposing a British or American default.
- Prefer the most exact natural word for the context. Use one stable term for one referent.
- Treat contractions, sentence-initial conjunctions, preposition endings, split infinitives, singular `they`, serial
  commas, possessives, quotation style, semicolons, dashes, colons, and fragments as contextual choices.

## Scientific and technical prose

- Preserve warranted hedging, epistemic modals, evidential distance, defined terms, citations, and functional passives
  or nominalizations.
- Apply useful STE-derived clarity without forcing controlled-language rules. Keep stable terminology, direct verbs,
  clear noun compounds, grammatical parallelism, and necessary field terminology.

## Translation

Translate communicative function rather than source word order. Preserve meaningful ambiguity, register, force,
emphasis, terminology, tense, modality, attribution, and negation scope. Do not introduce an arbitrary dialect shift.
