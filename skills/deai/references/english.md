# English de-AI calibration

Use these as review signals, not banned forms. Follow the source's genre, publication rules, and supplied voice first.

## Pattern clusters

- **Assistant framing** — Repeated helper preambles, option catalogs, generic reassurance, recap-and-offer closers, or
  self-narrating transitions that make a document sound like a chat response rather than its intended genre.
- **Announced insight** — Repeated `the key insight`, `the real question`, `what matters`, surprise announcements,
  performative emphasis, or lesson-like closers that announce importance instead of stating the supported point.
- **Mechanical balance** — Repeated negative pivots, forced groups of three, mirrored clauses, identical paragraph arcs,
  or symmetric pro/con framing when the evidence is not actually symmetric.
- **Mechanical cadence** — Runs of nearly identical sentence length, syntax, openings, clause order, fragments, colon
  reveals, or punctuation used for the same dramatic function.
- **Lexical drift** — Generic high-register verbs, ornamental modifiers, consultancy metaphors, padded transitions, and
  synonym cycling where a stable ordinary or domain-native term would be more exact.
- **False agency** — Abstract or inanimate subjects that hide a supplied material actor. Name the actor when the source
  provides one, but preserve established precise metonymy such as `the study shows` or `the court held`.
- **Performed register** — Slang, contractions, lowercase text, intimacy, or engineer jargon pasted onto an otherwise
  generic structure without support from the artifact or supplied voice.

## False positives

Do not flag prose merely because it is polished, formal, concise, grammatical, impersonal, or coherent. A semicolon, em
dash, passive construction, rhetorical question, three-part list, short sentence, long sentence, repeated technical term,
or conventional transition can be entirely natural. Preserve genuine balance, uncertainty, formality, repetition, and
cadence when they serve meaning or genre.
