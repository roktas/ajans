# English

Use this reference only when substantive English-specific judgment is material. Project, publication, genre, and house
rules take precedence.

## Grammar and meaning

- Keep grammatical subjects, modifiers, pronouns, comparisons, coordination, tense, modality, negation, and scope clear.
- Prefer a clear actor and working verb when they improve responsibility or comprehension. Keep passive voice and
  nominalization when the actor is unknown, irrelevant, deliberately backgrounded, or conventional in the genre.
- Preserve distinctions among fact, report, inference, possibility, permission, recommendation, obligation, and denial.
  Fluency is not a reason to strengthen or weaken a claim.

## Usage and terminology

- Preserve a consistent established English variety or the variety required by the publication; otherwise choose natural
  current English without imposing a British or American default.
- Prefer the most exact natural word for the context, not automatically the shortest or most elevated one.
- Use one stable term for one referent. Do not rotate through near-synonyms for variety when doing so blurs a technical or
  conceptual distinction.
- Treat contractions, sentence-initial conjunctions, preposition endings, split infinitives, singular `they`, serial
  commas, possessives, and quotation style as context-sensitive choices rather than universal prohibitions.
- Use semicolons, dashes, colons, fragments, and parallel structure for their real functions; do not add or remove them
  merely to satisfy a style signature.

## Scientific and technical prose

- Preserve warranted hedging, epistemic modals, evidential distance, defined terms, citations, and functional passives or
  nominalizations. Forms such as `may`, `might`, `could`, and `would` can carry scientific meaning and must not be
  normalized away merely for simplicity.
- Apply useful STE-derived clarity without forcing full STE: use stable terminology, prefer direct verbs over needless
  nominalization, unpack opaque noun chains, keep coordinate structures parallel, and remove wordiness that adds no
  precision.
- Keep field terminology that the audience needs. Explain it where the intended reader first needs help rather than
  replacing precise terms with generic plain-English substitutes.

## Translation

Translate communicative function rather than source word order. Preserve meaningful ambiguity, register, force,
emphasis, terminology, tense, modality, attribution, and negation scope. Do not introduce an arbitrary dialect shift.
