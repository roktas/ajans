# Turkish

Use this reference only when substantive Turkish-specific judgment is material. Project, publication, genre, and house
rules take precedence; otherwise use current Turkey Turkish appropriate to the audience and domain.

## Grammar and information structure

- Let information structure govern word order. Turkish often places focus near the predicate, but emphasis, contrast,
  rhythm, and genre can justify another order.
- Keep modifiers, relative clauses, and qualifiers close enough to their heads to avoid attachment ambiguity. Make case,
  possessive, and compound-noun relations recoverable instead of preserving English-shaped noun stacks.
- Check person, agreement, tense, aspect, mood, evidentiality, negation, and scope after every material rearrangement.
  Preserve distinctions among direct assertion, reported information, inference, possibility, obligation,
  recommendation, permission, and denial.
- Give pronouns, demonstratives, ellipsis, and omitted subjects clear referents while preserving deliberate omission or
  repetition when the relation remains clear.

## Expression and terminology

- Write Turkish relations rather than translating source word order. Prefer direct actors and verbs when they improve
  comprehension; keep passive and nominalized forms when procedure, scholarship, source status, or genre makes them
  functional.
- Prefer established project and domain terminology. Translate the contextual sense rather than forcing one universal
  equivalent, and repeat one stable term when it denotes one concept.
- Remove English-shaped padding, bureaucratic euphemism, and unsupported inflation only when meaning, evidential stance,
  register, and deliberate cadence remain intact.
- Treat spelling, punctuation, capitalization, headings, numerals, abbreviations, citations, semicolons, dashes, and
  parenthetical style as contextual publication decisions. Use a current authoritative source when a live usage decision
  matters.

## Scholarly prose

Preserve claim strength, attribution, evidential distance, defined terms, citations, and justified passives or
nominalizations. Do not replace a source-qualified claim with the narrator's assertion or make uncertainty disappear in
the name of directness.

## Translation

Translate intended function rather than visible word order. Preserve meaningful ambiguity, register, force, emphasis,
terminology, tense, modality, evidential source, and negation scope. When Turkish requires a distinction that the source
leaves genuinely open, resolve it only when surrounding evidence supports the choice.
